English translationAn origin essay on the Jiang (江) surname's descent and migrations. It opens with the principle that one must trace lineage to its source — as mountains spring from the Kunlun (崑崙) range and rivers from the essence of heaven, so people must trace back to their founding ancestor. The Jiang clan descends from the line of Bo Yi (伯益), enfeoffed at Jingyang (涇陽); the family took the place name Jiang as its surname. Later they lived at Jiyang (濟陽), which gave rise to the alternate clan-seat name 'Jiyang commandery.' Jingyang lies not far from the Huai (淮) and Xiang (湘) rivers — this was the starting point of the line's origin in Shandong. The line later passed to Jiangxi, to Raozhou (饒州), over more than fifty generations, down to the descendants of 'Sangu' (三古) and 'Wuzhai' (五齋). 'Sangu' refers to Wanli (萬里) and his two brothers, three men in all [the exact relationships here are somewhat [unclear] in the original]; 'Wuzhai' is named after one held in the tradition. Afterward they moved to Henan (豫) province and the Fujian–Guangdong (閩粵) region — all are this clan's offshoots.

English translationA textual-research note on the Jiang (江) surname, citing the comprehensive history 'Gangjian Daquan' (綱鑑大全). It traces the descent: a descendant of Zhuanxu [鬚/顓頊, here written 顯頃, likely a copyist variant] named Nü Xiu (女修) bore Da Ye (大業); Da Ye's wife Nü Hua (女莘) bore Gao [Yao]. English translationThe opening of a Jiang clan preface (江氏族序), with a heading naming Liujiu-lang (六九郎) and the noble-titled Qigong (貴七公), and the senior wife née Yan (顏氏, 'Daniang'), and a signed attribution to a respectful compiler of Xin'an (新安孝肅謹誌). The preface reflects that people, like water and trees, all have a source and a root, and like bamboo and pine all strive to bud and flourish; descendants who lose touch with their root cannot honor their distant ancestors nor secure their posterity. Thus all of one branch belong to the ancestors' unified line, and over countless generations the hope rests on later descendants carrying it forward. It alludes to the auspicious omens of the 'five cassia' (五桂) and 'three locust-trees' (三槐) foretelling high office, and exhorts the clan to strive. Tracing the clan's origin to high antiquity, it again identifies the founder as descended from Lord Bo Yi (伯益), enfeoffed at Jiang (江) and taking the state as the surname — continuing onto the next page.
